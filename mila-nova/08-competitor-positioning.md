# 8. Competitor Positioning & Market Analysis

## The market in plain terms

The AI-influencer space has three visible layers:

1. **Big virtual influencers** (brand-built characters with agencies behind
   them, e.g. the famous fashion-brand virtual models): premium, fully
   disclosed, monetised through brand deals — proof that audiences happily
   follow characters they know aren't real.
2. **AI glamour creators on subscription platforms** (the layer Mila enters):
   exploding since platforms like **Fanvue formally allowed disclosed AI
   creators** — Fanvue requires "AI-Generated" callouts on posts/profiles,
   bans any real-person likeness other than a verified account owner, and
   moderates AI content with a review process. Quality here ranges from
   genuinely premium character brands to mass-produced spam.
3. **The grey/black layer**: undisclosed AI passing as real women, deepfakes,
   celebrity lookalikes, "girlfriend" chatbots pretending to be human. This
   layer gets banned in waves, poisons buyer trust for everyone, and is
   exactly what Mila is positioned *against*.

## What works (observable patterns among successful AI creators)

- **A consistent, recognisable character.** Same face, same voice, same
  world, every post. Character consistency is the #1 visible difference
  between accounts that grow and accounts that churn content into the void.
- **Personality > polygon count.** Image quality is table stakes now. The
  accounts that retain subscribers have a *voice* people quote.
- **Interactivity.** Polls, fan-directed decisions, Q&As. Subscribers stay
  for participation, not for a folder of images.
- **A content *world*** (locations, routines, running jokes) that makes every
  post feel like an episode, not a render dump.
- **Reliable cadence.** Same drop days every week. Subscriptions are a
  promise of future content; keeping the schedule *is* the product.

## What looks spammy (and quietly kills accounts)

- Identical hyper-airbrushed "AI girl #4088" faces with no name, no world, no voice.
- Caption = 30 hashtags and a link. Nothing else.
- Comment-section link-dropping and mass DMs.
- "Check my profile for what I can't post here 🔞" bait — reads as scam, is
  usually a policy violation, and attracts exactly the buyers who'll be angry
  that explicit content doesn't exist.
- Pretending to be human ("my camera broke today!") — the audience always
  finds out, and the platforms increasingly detect it.
- Inconsistent face/body between posts — instantly reads low-effort.

## What buyers actually respond to

- **Honesty played as confidence.** "Not real. Still your favourite
  distraction" converts better than pretending, because the buyer doesn't
  have to suspend disbelief defensively — the joke includes them.
- **Being known.** Names on a Legends wall, their poll option winning,
  their theme suggestion getting made. Recognition is the real product.
- **Humour.** The overlap of "enjoys glamour content" and "enjoys a genuinely
  funny account" is massively underserved.
- **Predictability + surprise:** reliable schedule, surprising content.
- **Clear boundaries.** Counterintuitive but true: published rules make
  buyers *more* comfortable spending — nobody fears a bait-and-switch,
  in either direction.

## Risk register (each one can end the business — treat accordingly)

| Risk | Reality | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deepfakes / real-person likeness** | Illegal in a growing list of jurisdictions; instant platform ban; the single worst thing an AI creator can do | Never use real-person inputs; reverse-image-check the canon face; refuse all lookalike requests (template in [05](05-promotional-content.md)) |
| **Impersonation claims** | A real person may claim your generated face resembles them | Keep generation records proving synthetic origin; documented face-check at adoption + monthly spot checks; respond to any claim immediately and courteously |
| **Fake-intimacy harm** | Lonely users can over-attach to "girlfriend" experiences; regulators are watching AI-companion deception | Every automated message carries the AI-assist disclosure; no reciprocal-love claims; no meeting promises; a gentle template for users who over-attach ("I'm fiction, legend — and I'd genuinely rather you knew") |
| **Platform bans** | Any social account can vanish overnight, with or without cause | Email list from day one; content archive off-platform; never concentrate >50% of funnel on one social app; follow each platform's AI-label rules to the letter |
| **Payment processors** | Card networks are strict about adult-adjacent businesses; a processor cutoff = no revenue overnight | Stay genuinely non-explicit (not "technically compliant" — actually tame); accurate business descriptors; low refund/chargeback rate via fast support; let the platform (Fanvue) carry processing rather than DIY |
| **Age-gating failures** | Serving adult-adjacent content to minors = legal and platform catastrophe; age-verification laws are tightening in AU/UK/US | 18+ gate on the landing page; 18+ in every bio; platform-level age verification (another reason to use an established platform); block any user who states or appears under 18 |
| **Copyright/tool terms** | Some AI generators restrict commercial use of likeness-realistic outputs | Check the ToS of the specific generator for commercial rights before launch; keep receipts of paid plans |

## The moat, honestly

There is no technical moat — anyone can generate a pretty face. The durable
assets are: **the character's voice, the community habits (polls/rituals),
the email list, the compliance track record, and the back-catalogue.**
All five compound weekly and none can be copied by cloning your images.
