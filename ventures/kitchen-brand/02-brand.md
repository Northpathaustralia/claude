### The pick: **Whisk & Wink** ✅

Why it beats the rest:
- **Says everything in two words:** "Whisk" = kitchen credibility; "Wink" = the flirty,
  in-on-the-joke personality. No explanation needed.
- **The wink is a design asset:** logo mark, loading animation, sticker, sign-off
  ("— consider this your wink 😉"), a literal wink to camera as the sisters' video signature.
- **Tasteful ceiling:** "Wink" is playful without ever tipping into content that ad
  platforms flag. Saucy Sisters (the runner-up) is funnier but caps the brand at "saucy"
  and risks skirting ad-policy tonality; keep it as the *blog/series name* ("Saucy Sisters
  Sessions") so we get the joke without betting the domain on it.
- **Retail-ready:** works on packaging, private label, and a future physical line.
- Register **whiskandwink.com** + **whiskandwink.com.au**; handle `@whiskandwink` everywhere.

## 5. Final brand concept

**Positioning statement:** *For people who find cooking either boring or stressful,
Whisk & Wink is the kitchen gadget brand that makes it entertaining — every product is
chosen because it's genuinely useful AND satisfying to watch, and it's all demoed by two
sisters who feel like your funniest group chat.*

**Brand pillars:**
1. **Entertainment first.** If the demo doesn't make you smile or gasp, we don't stock it.
2. **Actually useful.** Every product kills a real, named annoyance. No gadget-drawer junk.
3. **Personality-led.** Lola & Mia front everything: site, ads, emails, packaging inserts.
4. **Honest & warm.** Real shipping times, real disclosure, no fake timers, no fake reviews.

**Voice:** cheeky best friend who happens to be great at cooking. Short sentences. Jokes
that land in one line. Flirty = confident and warm, never explicit, never at anyone's
expense. We tease the *products* and *each other*, not the customer.

**Voice examples:**
- Product blurb: "It dices an onion in one press. Your tears are officially unemployed."
- Shipping page: "We ship fast-ish. Tracked, always. Faster than Mia texts back, slower than Lola's comebacks."
- CTA buttons: "Add to cart, obviously" / "Treat your kitchen" / "Yes chef 🫡"

**What we never do:** fake scarcity timers, "90% OFF!!" pricing theatre, stolen UGC,
innuendo that couldn't run on daytime TV, punching down, or pretending to be something
we're not (see disclosure policy in `10-risks-roadmap.md`).

## 6. The two sisters

> **Presentation policy (read first):** Lola and Mia are written, shot, and voiced as
> completely human — real chemistry, real flaws, real running jokes. They are, factually,
> AI-generated virtual creators, and the brand handles that the way the top virtual
> influencers do: we never claim they're flesh-and-blood people, the About/Meet page
> carries one charming line of disclosure, and any ad containing photorealistic AI people
> uses the platform's AI-content label (Meta and TikTok both require this). This is not a
> handicap — audiences demonstrably don't care once they love the characters, and it makes
> the brand un-cancellable and ad-account-safe. What makes them feel real is *writing and
> consistency*, not concealment: fixed appearance, fixed voices, continuity (they remember
> last week's video), imperfection (burnt garlic bread stays in the cut).

### LOLA — the bold one (blonde, 23)
- **Personality:** confident, quick, a little chaotic, allergic to boring. The one who
  slams the chopper too hard and says "as advertised." Flirts with the *camera*, not
  with anyone in particular — eyebrow raised, always in on the joke.
- **Speaking style:** punchlines, dares, mock-drama. "Babe. BABE. Look at this dice."
  Calls the audience "babe" and "chef." Says "obsessed" ironically and unironically.
- **Visual style:** blonde, effortless-glam; gold hoops, claw clip, oversized shirt or
  slip dress with an apron thrown over ("dressed for the bar, cooking at home" energy).
  Everyday-model gorgeous, styled like a creator, not a catalogue.
- **Role in the brand:** hype, hooks, comedy, the first 3 seconds of every video, the
  "buy it, thank me later" energy. Fronts the cheeky campaigns and the sales moments.
- **Catchphrases:** "Consider that a wink." · "As advertised." · "I don't gatekeep, I
  girl-boss." · "That's dinner, baby."
- **How she talks about product (Chop Boss):** "One press. ONE. I've cried over onions
  for the last time and honestly? Character development."

### MIA — the clever one (brunette, 21)
- **Personality:** sweet, sharp, secretly the funnier one (deadpan). The competence to
  Lola's chaos. Reads the manual, tests the claims, calls Lola out with one look. Charm
  that sneaks up on you.
- **Speaking style:** calm, precise, devastating asides. "It's giving… actually useful."
  Explains the *why* ("fixed 15-degree angle — that's why it works"). Ends demos with a
  tiny smile like she knows she just sold you.
- **Visual style:** brunette, soft-girl polish; slick bun or waves, gold pendant, knit tank
  + linen apron. Clean-girl aesthetic, warm and approachable — the sister your mum trusts.
- **Role in the brand:** trust, demos, education, FAQ answers, review responses, email
  voice for post-purchase and how-to content. Fronts the "does it actually work?" tests.
- **Catchphrases:** "Tested so you don't have to." · "Small tool. Big opinion." ·
  "Lola, read the instructions." · "…and that's why it works."
- **How she talks about product (RollSharp):** "Your knives aren't bad, they're neglected.
  Fifteen-degree fixed angle, diamond disc, sixty seconds. Watch the tomato. *Watch the tomato.*"

### The dynamic
Lola hooks, Mia proves. Lola oversells for laughs ("this changed my personality"), Mia
corrects with the real benefit ("it changed her *prep time*, by nine minutes"). Running
bits: Mia timing Lola's claims with a stopwatch; Lola "borrowing" Mia's gadgets; the
sisters' mum occasionally reviewing products via text message (screenshot content); the
end-of-video synchronized wink — the brand signature.

### Boundaries (tasteful + ad-safe, hard rules)
- Flirty means confident, warm, playful. **Never** explicit, suggestive framing of body
  parts, lingerie/swimwear in kitchen content, innuendo about the products, or "girlfriend
  experience" tonality. Test: would this run in a daytime TV ad break? If no, cut it.
- No alcohol-centric content (ad targeting restrictions), no health claims, no "burns
  calories" claims for the oil sprayer, no dissing competitors by name.
- Wardrobe: stylish and flattering, standard fashion-brand level. Attractive through
  charisma, styling, and lighting — the humour is never "look at us," it's "look at *this*."
- They are adults with adult wit, but the account must be safe for a 16-year-old's feed —
  that's also what keeps CPMs low and reach broad.
- The characters never DM flirtatiously, never respond to sexualized comments except with
  comedy deflection ("sir, this is a *chopper* demo"), and comment moderation hides
  explicit replies (keeps the ad account's engagement signals clean).
