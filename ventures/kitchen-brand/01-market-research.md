# 2–3. Market Research & Product Recommendations

## Market research findings

**Niche health.** Kitchen/home is consistently a top-3 dropshipping category worldwide.
Demand is evergreen (everyone cooks), spikes reliably (Christmas, Mother's Day, wedding
season, New Year "meal prep era"), and the products demo brilliantly on video — which is
what actually matters now, because TikTok/Reels distribution decides winners.

**What's working in kitchen dropshipping right now:**
- **"Satisfying demo" products** — one clean 3-second visual payoff (a chopper slamming
  through an onion, a sharpener restoring a dead knife). These carry themselves in ads.
- **Problem-annihilators** — products that kill a *specific, universal* annoyance: crying
  over onions, burnt-hand pot draining, stale chip bags, garlic fingers.
- **"Why didn't I know this existed?"** gadgets — novelty + genuine utility. Pure novelty
  (gag gifts) has high returns; utility-novelty converts and sticks.
- **Small electric upgrades of manual chores** — mini choppers, electric grinders, spin
  scrubbers. "Electric version of a boring thing" is a proven angle.
- **Giftability** — kitchen gadgets are the default "I don't know what to get them" gift.
  Q4 can be 40%+ of annual revenue. Bundles as gifts lift AOV hard.

**What loses:** heavy/fragile items (cookware, glass), anything food-contact from unverified
suppliers without certification (safety + trust), ultra-cheap items under ~$15 sell price
(can't afford ads), appliances with plugs/voltage issues (AU/US plug mismatch, warranty
pain), knives-as-knives (ad policy friction — sharpeners are fine, blade glorification isn't).

**Customer profile (primary):** women 22–45, home cooks and meal-preppers, TikTok/IG heavy,
buy on impulse under ~A$60, motivated by (in order) entertainment → "solves my exact
annoyance" → gift-giving. Secondary: men 25–45 buying gifts, and the "kitchen tech" guy.

**Competitive gap:** every competitor sells the same AliExpress catalogue with the same
white-background photos and fake-urgency timers. Nobody in the space has a *face*, let
alone two. Personality + consistency is the entire moat. (Reference points: virtual
creators like Lil Miquela prove disclosed non-human personas can carry major brands;
kitchen creators like Nara Smith / "that couple who reviews gadgets" prove kitchen content
prints reach.)

**Sourcing note:** all products below are abundantly available on CJdropshipping, Zendrop
and AliExpress with AU/US warehouses for the top sellers. Costs shown are typical landed
cost ranges (product + shipping) in AUD; verify per-supplier before launch.

## The product line — 12 researched picks (top 10 get full product pages)

### 1. The 14-in-1 Multi Chopper — "Chop Boss"
- **Problem it solves:** slow, messy, dangerous knife work; crying over onions; salad prep taking 30 minutes.
- **Why people buy:** the demo is hypnotic — a whole onion becomes perfect dice in one press. Saves real time daily.
- **Buyer:** meal preppers, busy parents, students, anyone who hates chopping.
- **Pain points:** time, mess, uneven cuts, onion tears, knife fear.
- **Emotional trigger:** competence + satisfaction ("my prep, but on easy mode").
- **Ad hooks:** "POV: you stopped crying over onions in 2026." / "I meal prepped a week of food in 11 minutes." / Lola slams the lid, Mia flinches.
- **Bundles:** + herb scissors + snap strainer = Meal Prep Bundle.
- **Cost:** A$12–18 · **Sell:** A$44.95–49.95.
- **Beats competitors because:** we demo it with recipes + jokes, not stock footage; we include a quick-start card and blade guide (PDF) competitors don't.
- **Objections → answers:** "hard to clean" → dishwasher-safe, 20-sec rinse demo; "blades dull" → stainless, replaceable; "too bulky" → nests flat, show drawer shot.
- **Upsell/cross-sell:** blade refill pack, herb scissors, storage containers.

### 2. Rolling Knife Sharpener — "RollSharp"

- **Why people buy:** the tomato-slice before/after is one of the highest-converting demos in the niche. Feels like a premium tool, not a gadget.
- **Buyer:** home cooks 25–55, men strongly over-index, top gift item.
- **Pain:** squashing tomatoes, sawing bread, "I know my knives are bad but sharpening is intimidating."
- **Trigger:** transformation + mastery ("restaurant-sharp in 60 seconds").
- **Hooks:** "Your knives aren't bad. They're just neglected." / paper-slice test / "Dad gift that isn't socks."

- **Cost:** A$14–22 · **Sell:** A$59.95–69.95.
- **Beats competitors:** premium positioning + on-camera 15° vs 20° angle education = trust.
- **Objections:** "will it wreck my knives?" → diamond disc, fixed angle, safer than pull-throughs; "my knives are cheap anyway" → cheap knives benefit most.
- **Upsell:** magnetic knife strip, cut-resistant glove.
- **Ad note:** frame as *sharpening/maintenance*, never blade glorification — keeps Meta/TikTok happy.

## Selection logic (why these, not random gadgets)

Every pick scores 4+/5 on all of: visual demo value, universal pain point, ≥60% gross
margin at target price, <500g light/unbreakable shipping, low return risk (no sizing, no
fragility, no plug/voltage issues except USB-C which is universal). Items 1, 2, 4, 5, 9
are the five with proven long-run ad performance in this niche; 3, 6, 8, 11 are AOV
builders; 7, 10, 12 are content engines that feed the sisters' comedy format.

**Deliberately excluded:** knife sets (ad policy + shipping), cookware (weight/damage),
anything glass-heavy except the oil sprayer (small, well-packed), single-use gag gadgets
(banana slicers etc. — views without conversions), and food-contact items without
verifiable food-grade certification from the supplier (always request the cert PDF).
