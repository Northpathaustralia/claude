The Invisible Backpack

A story about kindness, empathy and the things we cannot see.

In a warm little forest lives a tiny echidna named Pip — a little friend who notices things others sometimes miss. One morning, Pip discovers that every animal is carrying a backpack nobody can see… and that kindness makes backpacks lighter.

For children aged 3–7 · Written by James Forycki

Meet the friends

Pip

A tiny echidna with big brown eyes and an even bigger heart. Pip notices things others sometimes miss — especially how his friends are feeling.

Birdie

A little blue bird with a sweet song, who learns that being brave can start with one tiny note.

Koala

Gentle and quiet, Koala carries the heaviest invisible backpack of all — and shows readers that "I'm fine" isn't always the whole story.

Wombat & Kangaroo

Wombat's hard day feels lighter with a friend's help — and Kangaroo's big bright smile reminds us that even happy faces can be carrying something.

For parents & teachers

The Invisible Backpack is a curriculum-connected classroom resource, written to open gentle conversations about empathy, kindness, emotional awareness, hidden feelings and belonging. The book ends with a discussion page including questions like:

  • What is something you might keep in an invisible backpack?
  • How can you tell if someone's backpack is heavy?
  • What can you do to help someone feel lighter?
  • When do you feel most like Pip?

About the author

James Forycki is an Australian author who writes gentle, heartfelt stories for young children. Inspired by the animals and quiet corners of the Australian bush, James writes about the small feelings little people carry — and the small kindnesses that make them lighter. The Invisible Backpack is his debut picture book.

Frequently asked questions

What age is this book for?

Children aged roughly 3–7 — preschool to early primary. The sentences are short and warm, and the illustrations carry the feeling on every page.

What is an "invisible backpack"?

It's the story's gentle picture for the feelings, worries and hard days everyone carries — even when nobody can see them. The book helps children name those feelings and respond to others with kindness.

Is it an Australian book?

Very! Pip is an echidna, and his friends are a koala, kangaroo, wombat and a little blue bird, in a watercolour Australian bush full of gum trees and wattle.
