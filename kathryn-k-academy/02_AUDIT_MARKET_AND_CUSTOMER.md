## PART B — ROUND 1: THE CUSTOMER PROBLEM

### Customer 1 — "Aspiring Aimee" (primary, Tiers 1–2)
18–34, Australian, loves makeup, wants to become a paid MUA or at least stop wasting money on products she can't use properly.

- **Problem:** Cannot bridge the gap between YouTube tutorials (free, fragmented, not structured, wrong for her face/climate) and formal beauty college ($1,500–$5,000, VERIFIED, often campus-based, slow).
- **Severity/frequency:** Every application; blocks a career decision. ESTIMATED strong — beauty education searches and course providers proliferate at these prices, which only happens where demand exists. RVAL: confirm via Kat's DMs and validation sprint.
- **Current solutions & weaknesses:** free YouTube (no structure, no feedback, no path to income); TAFE/colleges (expensive, inflexible); big online academies like QC (generic, foreign-taught, no Australian business context, no live feedback, no real artist relationship).
- **The unmet piece nobody sells well:** "how to actually make money as an MUA **in Australia**" — ABN, GST thresholds, insurance, pricing, contracts, kit-building on a budget. VERIFIED gap: mainstream course listings emphasise technique and kits, not Australian freelance business setup.

### Customer 2 — "Working Wendy" (Tier 3)
Qualified or self-taught artist, 1–5 years in, earning inconsistently. Wants pro-level skills (editorial, SFX), confidence to charge more, and a mentor who is visibly working in the industry.

- **Problem:** Skill ceiling + pricing ceiling; SFX training is scarce outside capital-city colleges. Severity high — this is her income. ESTIMATED/RVAL.
- Professionals experience the problem differently from home users: they need advanced technique, speed, kit hygiene at scale, and business systems — this justifies a separate flagship tier rather than one mega-course.

### Is the problem commercial? 
**Yes — with evidence.** People already pay Kat to teach (VERIFIED); competitors sustain $1,198–$2,999 price points (VERIFIED); the Australian-business-setup angle is visibly underserved (VERIFIED gap in competitor curricula). Final confirmation comes from the validation sprint, not this document.

### Price-band map (VERIFIED range)

- $2,400–5,000: advanced/campus certificates ← Tier 3 sits here on value, undercutting campus prices while adding live access

### Regulatory note (VERIFIED — shapes all copy)
Only RTOs may deliver **accredited** training, and implying accreditation is an offence ([ASQA](https://www.asqa.gov.au/course-accreditation/overview); [NOC guide](https://noc.edu.au/is-online-training-legit-in-australia-heres-when-its-legal-and-when-its-not/)). Rules for this business: never use "nationally recognised," "accredited," or bare "Certificate III/IV"-style naming; issue "Certificate of Completion — Kathryn K Academy (non-accredited professional training)"; add a plain-English FAQ entry. Makeup artistry is not a licensed occupation in QLD (ESTIMATED — confirm with lawyer), so non-accredited training is the industry norm; this is a compliance point, not a business blocker.
