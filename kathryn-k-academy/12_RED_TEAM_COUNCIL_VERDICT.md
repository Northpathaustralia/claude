## ROUND 19 — RED-TEAM ATTACK

Each weakness: explained → severity rated → consequence → correction → strategy updated.

### 1. Customer demand is still assumed, not proven
**Explain:** Everything past "Kat already sells lessons informally" is inference from competitor pricing and category logic, not confirmed demand for *this specific* product ladder. **Severity: HIGH.** **Consequence:** building Tier 2 in full before real presale evidence would waste the Budget 3 spend. **Correction:** this is already handled — `04_VALIDATION_PLAN.md` gates all real spend behind presale evidence. **Update:** none needed, the plan already defends against this; flagged here to confirm the defense is real, not decorative.

### 2. Differentiation ("AU business content") could be copied fast
**Explain:** Once Kat's course proves the business-setup angle sells, larger academies (QC, Australian Beauty School) could add a similar module within a quarter. **Severity: MEDIUM.** **Consequence:** the white-space advantage in doc 02 could shrink over 12–18 months. **Correction:** Kat's actual personal brand and real client relationships are the harder-to-copy moat, not the curriculum topic list — lean marketing (doc 08) harder into founder-led content, not just curriculum claims. **Update:** doc 08's content pillars already weight "founder/behind-the-scenes" and "real demonstration" heavily — this is confirmed as the right emphasis, not a gap.

### 3. Kat is the sole point of failure (key-person risk)
**Explain:** every tier depends on Kat's live time (filming, Q&A, seminars, critiques). Illness, burnout, or simply not enjoying the teaching workload at scale breaks the model. **Severity: HIGH.** **Consequence:** at Scenario 3–4 volumes (doc 06), her personal capacity is very plausibly exceeded — cohort caps (20–30) exist for exactly this reason but growth pressure could push against them. **Correction:** treat the Tier 3 cohort cap as genuinely non-negotiable, not a soft target; build a "pause/no new cohort" decision rule into the monthly ops review (doc 07); do not let revenue ambition override Kat's actual bandwidth. **Update:** added explicitly to doc 07's monthly task list as a required check.

### 4. Pricing is benchmarked, not tested
**Explain:** the $199/$799/$2,995 price points come from competitor comparison (doc 02), not from actual willingness-to-pay data for Kat specifically. **Severity: MEDIUM.** **Consequence:** presale could underperform if her specific audience has different price sensitivity than the broader market. **Correction:** the founding-cohort discount (doc 04/11) already functions as a live price test — if founding conversion is weak at $649, drop to $499 for the *next* cohort rather than assuming the whole tier is dead. **Update:** doc 04's IMPROVE branch should explicitly include "re-test at a lower price point" — added.

### 5. Manufacturing/copyability risk was correctly avoided, not solved
**Explain:** deferring Lash Lock doesn't answer whether it's a good physical product — it just delays the question. **Severity: LOW (for now).** **Consequence:** none until Year 2. **Correction:** none needed yet; re-open Round 3/4-style scoring for physical product sub-variants when Year 2 planning starts. **Update:** flagged in handover doc as an acknowledged gap already — consistent treatment.

### 6. Margins assume no major platform fee increases or chargebacks
**Explain:** doc 06's ~92–95% margins assume clean Stripe processing; course businesses see real chargeback/refund rates (industry pattern, ESTIMATED 3–8% for online education). **Severity: MEDIUM.** **Consequence:** Scenario 2+ profit figures could be overstated by several percentage points. **Correction:** add a 5% refund/chargeback allowance to the financial model. **Update:** doc 06 should be read with this correction — net margins effectively ~87–90% rather than ~92–95%; directionally the same conclusion (very high margin), doesn't change the GO logic.

### 7. Competition from free content is undercounted
**Explain:** TikTok/YouTube free tutorials are the biggest real competitor for Tier 1 specifically, not the paid academies in doc 02's table. **Severity: MEDIUM.** **Consequence:** Tier 1 conversion could be lower than modeled if free content satisfies casual browsers. **Correction:** Tier 1's value prop must lean harder on structure + feedback + certificate + upgrade path, not just "technique," since technique alone is freely available. **Update:** doc 03's Tier 1 description already includes structure/community/certificate — confirmed adequate, but marketing copy (doc 10) should keep foregrounding "structured path," not "learn makeup" generically.

### 8. Organic growth assumptions in doc 06 are optimistic
**Explain:** 10–20%/month follower growth and viral-moment dependency in Scenario 3–4 are stated as required but not demonstrated as achievable for this specific account. **Severity: MEDIUM.** **Consequence:** Scenarios 3–4 may simply not happen even with excellent execution — this is a market/luck factor, not just an effort factor. **Correction:** already handled by explicit "what must be true" framing — no further correction needed, just discipline in communicating this honestly to Kat (see presentation doc, Round 21).

### 9. Revenue projections could be misread as promises
**Explain:** presenting a $1M scenario in an emotional anniversary context risks it landing as an expectation rather than a ceiling. **Severity: HIGH (relationship risk, not just financial).** **Consequence:** pressure on Kat, disappointment if Scenario 1–2 (the realistic range) is what actually happens. **Correction:** the anniversary presentation (doc 13) must lead with Scenario 1–2 as the real plan and frame Scenario 4 explicitly as "if lightning strikes," never as the pitch. **Update:** locked as a hard requirement for doc 13.

### 10. Founder roles: verbal intent vs. written agreement gap
**Explain:** James-sole-director/Kat-public-face is currently just stated intent (doc 07); no agreement exists. **Severity: HIGH.** **Consequence:** real legal and relationship exposure the longer this stays informal, especially once real revenue exists. **Correction:** already flagged as non-negotiable in doc 07 — confirmed here as the single highest-priority non-product task in the whole plan, ahead of Tier 3 build-out if there's ever a scheduling conflict.

**Overall red-team conclusion:** no finding here invalidates the plan. The most serious items (#3 key-person risk, #9 expectation-setting, #10 written agreement) are about *how the plan is run and communicated*, not about whether it should be run at all. All three corrections have been folded into docs 07, 04, and 13 respectively.

## ROUND 20 — FINAL COMMERCIAL VERDICT

# GO AFTER VALIDATION

### Why not a plain GO
Real demand for *this specific* product ladder at *these specific* prices is not yet proven — only proven-adjacent (existing lesson sales, category pricing benchmarks). Spending Budget 3 money before the validation sprint closes would violate the plan's own core discipline.

### Why not PIVOT or DO NOT PROCEED
The underlying evidence is genuinely strong: real existing demand for Kat's teaching, a real underserved curriculum gap (AU business setup), high-margin low-risk delivery mechanics, and a founder team with directly relevant skills (James: funnels/systems/finance; Kat: the actual expertise and audience). No red-team finding was severe enough to justify stopping — every High-severity item has a concrete, already-applied correction.

- **Brand:** Kathryn K Academy (fallback: The Artist Standard)
- **Product:** 3-tier course ladder — Beginner's Blueprint ($199) → Working Artist Kickstart ($799, founding $649) → Master Artistry Program ($2,995/VIP $3,995)
- **Customer:** Aspiring Aimee (T1/T2), Working Wendy (T3)
- **Price:** as above, instalment/payment-plan option to be added before full launch (Round 18 correction)
- **Prototype/production method:** phone + lav mic + key light, self-filmed by Kat, batch production per doc 03
- **Validation method:** the 4-instrument sprint in doc 04, with the pricing-retest amendment from Round 19 #4
- **Marketing strategy:** organic-first per doc 08, founder-led content weighted heavily per Round 19 #2
- **Budget:** 3-tier budget in doc 06, day-by-day spend plan in doc 11 — realistic total spend to the GO/STOP decision point is under $800
- **Launch model:** validation-gated, presale-funded first cohort (content filmed as it's taught)
- **Ownership model:** James sole director/owner initially, written Founder & Brand Agreement with Kat within 60 days of any real revenue — treated as non-negotiable per Round 19 #10
- **90-day plan:** doc 11, in full
