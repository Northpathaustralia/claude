## LESSON 5 — Apply (A): eyeshadow the clean way
- **Objective:** simple 3-shade placement that reads polished, no advanced blending.
- **Duration:** ~7 min · **Products:** neutral trio (transition/lid/definer), fluffy + flat brush, Lash Lock held for control · **Setup:** Set 1 over-shoulder + macro.

**Presenter script:**
> "Three shades, three jobs — that's all a clean eye needs. A soft transition shade in the crease to blur, a shimmer or matte on the lid for light, and a slightly deeper shade in the outer corner for shape. That's it. No sixteen colours.
> Brush discipline is the secret: load a *little*, tap off the excess — that alone halves your fallout — and build in thin layers. Transition first, small circles in the crease, windscreen-wiper motion, keep it above the fold so it doesn't smudge. Then pat the lid colour on flat — patting deposits, sweeping loses it to fallout. Then just the outer corner deeper, and blend the edge with the leftover on your fluffy brush.
> Keep your control tool up while you pack the lid — that's when fallout happens most. Tap off, pat on, protect. Clean eye, every time."

- **Demonstration:** transition → lid pat → outer corner → soft blend; tool held during lid-packing.
- **Camera:** over-shoulder placement + macro of tap-off and pat.
- **Close-ups:** brush tap-off; patting motion; finished clean lid.
- **B-roll:** heavy-load-vs-tapped fallout compare.
- **On-screen text:** "TAP OFF = less fallout" · "PAT, don't sweep" · 3-shade map.
- **Graphic callouts:** STEP 4 · APPLY; labelled eye diagram.
- **Editing:** on-screen eye map overlay; hold on pat macro.
- **Music/sound:** calm; keep brush sounds.
- **Continuity:** same eye carried to Lesson 6.
- **Safety/claim:** none beyond ✅ tool language.
- **Thumbnail:** "3 shades, clean eye". **Export:** clean+captioned. **QC:** placement legible; tap-off shown.

## LESSON 6 — Clean liner & lashes without the print
- **Objective:** even-ish liner + mascara with no lid print.
- **Duration:** ~6 min · **Products:** liner (pencil/gel/pen), mascara, Lash Lock/guard · **Setup:** Set 1 over-shoulder + macro.

**Presenter script:**
> "Liner and mascara are where clean eyes go to die — smudges, prints, uneven wings. Fix one: don't try to draw one long line. Dot along the lash line first, then connect the dots — your hand follows a path instead of freehanding. For a little wing, line up the angle with your lower lash line and keep it small; you can always build it.
> Mascara print on your lid or brow bone? Hold your control tool behind the lashes as a backstop — wiggle the mascara at the root, and the tool catches the print instead of your skin. Do the top from underneath, then behind for the tips. Lower lashes: tool under, so nothing dots your fresh under-eye. Even, clean, no raccoon."

- **Demonstration:** dot-and-connect liner; small wing angle; mascara with tool backstop top & bottom.
- **Camera:** over-shoulder + macro of tool-as-backstop.
- **Close-ups:** dotting; connect; mascara backstop catch.
- **B-roll:** lid-print with vs without backstop.
- **On-screen text:** "Dot → connect" · "Tool = backstop" · "No lid print".
- **Graphic callouts:** angle guide line for wing.
- **Editing:** side-by-side print vs clean.
- **Music/sound:** calm.
- **Continuity:** finished eye → Lesson 8.
- **Safety/claim:** ⚠️CLAIM-GATE mascara/backstop claim (verify test #3); honest that steadiness improves with practice — no "perfect wings guaranteed."
- **Thumbnail:** "No more lid print". **Export:** clean+captioned. **QC:** backstop visibly catches; honest framing.

## LESSON 7 — The 5 mistakes that make it look messy (+ rescue)
- **Objective:** diagnose & fix the common failures (retention + trust).
- **Duration:** ~6 min · **Products:** as needed for quick fixes · **Setup:** Set 1 medium + macro inserts.

**Presenter script:**
> "Five mistakes, five fixes. One: too much product — you can't clean up your way out of a heavy hand, so tap off and build. Two: skipping the set-before-shadow step, so fallout grabs wet concealer — set first. Three: swiping concealer into your lines — press it. Four: no lid base, so shadow creases by lunch. Five: dragging fallout across your face to 'fix' it — instead, sweep it *off* dry with a clean fluffy brush, or lift it with the sticky side of tape, *then* touch up. Never wipe wet.
> If it's already gone wrong: don't add more. Take a clean brush, buff the edges, press a touch of concealer over any fallout — press, don't wipe — and re-set. Nine times out of ten you can rescue it in under a minute without starting over."

- **Demonstration:** re-create each mistake briefly, then the fix; the dry-sweep/tape rescue.
- **Camera:** medium + macro of each fix.
- **Close-ups:** dry-brush fallout sweep; press-over rescue.
- **B-roll:** messy → rescued in one shot.
- **On-screen text:** numbered "MISTAKE 1…5 / FIX".
- **Graphic callouts:** big number stamps.
- **Editing:** snappy problem→fix cuts; hold the rescue.
- **Music/sound:** slightly brighter (diagnostic energy).
- **Continuity:** —
- **Safety/claim:** none; tape near eye = "gently, on cheek fallout only," safety note on screen.
- **Thumbnail:** "5 mistakes ruining your eyes". **Export:** clean+captioned (also strong organic cut). **QC:** each fix clear; safety note present.

## LESSON 8 — Put it together: real-time run + does it hold in the heat?
- **Objective:** confidence via a full real-time run; prove longevity (AU angle).
- **Duration:** ~7 min · **Products:** full essentials · **Setup:** Set 1 real-time single run + a Set-3 "heat check" outro.

**Presenter script:**
> "Let's do the whole thing, real time, no cuts hiding the boring bits, so you see it's genuinely quick. Prep — press, set. Base — thin, set. Control tool up. Transition, tap off, wiper. Lid, pat. Outer corner, blend. Liner — dot, connect. Mascara — backstop, top then behind. Lower lashes, tool under. Done. That's a clean, done eye in about [X] minutes.
> Now the real test — does it hold? I filmed myself after a full day out on the Gold Coast, [X hours, ~30°C]. Here's the check: [footage]. Still clean, still in place, under-eyes still smooth. That's the point of the whole method — not just pretty for the mirror, but makeup that behaves all day, in our weather. You've got this. Print your method card, do three practice runs this week, and film yourself on day seven — you'll see the jump."

- **Demonstration:** uninterrupted real-time run; end-of-day heat-check footage.
- **Camera:** locked medium for the run (speed it slightly in edit but show it's continuous); Set-3 selfie-style heat check.
- **Close-ups:** a few macro inserts without breaking the "real-time" feel.
- **B-roll:** timestamped heat check; before/after of the day.
- **On-screen text:** running step tags; "Real time" badge; "[X]h · [X]°C — still clean".
- **Graphic callouts:** timer; heat-check stamp.
- **Editing:** keep continuity obvious; resist over-cutting.
- **Music/sound:** warm build; drop for the heat-check reveal.
- **Continuity:** same look start→heat check.
- **Safety/claim:** ⚠️CLAIM-GATE longevity — only state the *actual* hours/temp you really filmed; no "lasts all day guaranteed."
- **Thumbnail:** "Does it hold in the heat?". **Export:** clean+captioned + heat-check as standalone organic. **QC:** run reads continuous; heat-check honest & real.
