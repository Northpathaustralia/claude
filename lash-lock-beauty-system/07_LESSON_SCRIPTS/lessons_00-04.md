## LESSON 0 — Welcome + The Clean Method in 60 seconds
- **Objective:** orient her; make her feel capable; give the whole method up front.
- **Duration:** ~3 min · **Products:** none (or method card on screen) · **Setup:** Set 1, Kat talking-head, signature makeup.

**Presenter script:**
> "If your eyeshadow keeps falling and wrecking your under-eyes — or your makeup looks great in the mirror and messy in photos — this is the class for you. I'm Kat, I'm a working makeup artist on the Gold Coast, and I'm going to give you the exact method I use so your eyes look clean, done, and like *you* did them on purpose.
> Here's the whole thing in one breath, so you're not lost: **Prep, Base, Control, Apply, Set.** Five steps. Prep the skin so nothing creases. Base the lid so shadow grips. Control the fallout so it never touches your under-eyes. Apply the colour the clean way. Set it so it holds — even in our heat.
> That's it. Each lesson is short, one step at a time. Grab the one-page method card in your downloads — print it, stick it on your mirror. Let's start with the tools you actually need, because it's way fewer than you think."

- **Demonstration actions:** none; hold the printed method card to lens at "grab the card."
- **Camera:** eye-level medium; slow 5% push-in on the 5-step line. **Close-ups:** none.
- **B-roll:** 2–3s clean-side eye result to open (from a later shoot); method-card insert.
- **On-screen text:** "PREP · BASE · CONTROL · APPLY · SET" appears word-by-word as spoken.
- **Graphic callouts:** 5-step tag row builds.
- **Editing:** open on the eye result (hook), then title, then Kat. No logo before value.
- **Music/sound:** warm bed, ducked; small "settle" whoosh as steps stack.
- **Continuity:** lock outfit/makeup as batch reference.
- **Safety/claim:** none spoken about the tool yet.
- **Thumbnail text:** "The 5-step clean eye". **Export:** clean master + captioned. **QC:** steps legible sound-off; audio above music; no over-claim.

## LESSON 2 — Prep (P): skin & eye base that won't ruin later
- **Objective:** stop creasing/cakey under-eyes before they start.
- **Duration:** ~6 min · **Products:** moisturiser, concealer, setting powder, sponge/brush · **Setup:** Set 1 over-shoulder mirror + macro insert. Model: Kat or a consented model incl. one mature/hooded shot.

**Presenter script:**
> "Ninety percent of 'my under-eyes look cakey' is a prep problem, not a product problem. So: moisturise and let it sink in — if you apply concealer onto wet moisturiser, it slides and creases. Wait sixty seconds; do your brows while you wait.
> Under-eye concealer: less than you think. One thin layer, pressed — not swiped — with a damp sponge. Swiping drags it into your lines. Pressing sets it *into* the skin.
> Now the step everyone skips: set it lightly with powder *before* you do your eyeshadow. Why? Because in a minute we're going to do shadow, and if fallout lands on un-set concealer it grabs and smears. Set it now, thank yourself later. On mature or hooded eyes, use even less powder and only where it creases — powder into deep lines makes them look deeper."

- **Demonstration:** moisturise → wait (brows) → press concealer with damp sponge → light powder set. Show the mature/hooded tweak.
- **Camera:** over-shoulder for placement; macro insert for the press-not-swipe; medium for the "why".
- **Close-ups:** damp-sponge pressing; powder tap-off before touching skin.
- **B-roll:** creased vs smooth under-eye comparison (from a longevity shoot).
- **On-screen text:** "PRESS, don't swipe" · "SET before shadow".
- **Graphic callouts:** STEP 1 · PREP tag.
- **Editing:** hold on the press macro; split-screen swipe-vs-press if available.
- **Music/sound:** calm bed; keep sponge sounds.
- **Continuity:** same concealer/powder as Lessons 4–8.
- **Safety/claim:** no therapeutic claims; "helps it not crease," not "erases wrinkles."
- **Thumbnail:** "Why your under-eyes crease". **Export:** clean+captioned. **QC:** press vs swipe unmistakable; mature tweak included.

## LESSON 3 — Base (B): make shadow grip & stop creasing
- **Objective:** longevity + payoff via a lid base.
- **Duration:** ~5 min · **Products:** eye primer or concealer + powder, flat brush · **Setup:** Set 1 over-shoulder + macro.

**Presenter script:**
> "Eyeshadow creasing or fading by lunch? Your lid needs a base. Two ways: a proper eye primer, or — if you don't own one — a *tiny* bit of concealer set with a *tiny* bit of powder. Either gives the shadow something to grab so it stays put and the colour shows up truer.
> Key word is tiny. Too much base and it creases faster, not slower. Thin, even, then a whisper of powder so it's not tacky. On oily lids, primer wins; on mature/hooded lids, keep it sheer so it doesn't gather in the fold. Base done — now we protect everything before a single flake of shadow."

- **Demonstration:** apply thin base, set, tap-test tackiness. Show oily vs hooded note.
- **Camera:** over-shoulder + macro of the thin even layer.
- **Close-ups:** brush laying base; powder whisper.
- **B-roll:** creased-shadow vs clean-shadow end-of-day compare.
- **On-screen text:** "Base = shadow grips + lasts" · "TINY".
- **Graphic callouts:** STEP 2 · BASE.
- **Editing:** quick before/after crease compare.
- **Music/sound:** calm.
- **Continuity:** same lid state carried into Lesson 4/5.
- **Safety/claim:** none.
- **Thumbnail:** "Stop shadow creasing". **Export:** clean+captioned. **QC:** "tiny" emphasised; alt (concealer) shown.

## LESSON 4 — Control (C): catch fallout with Lash Lock ★ HERO LESSON
- **Objective:** the signature moment — show fallout being caught before it ruins under-eyes.
- **Duration:** ~6 min · **Products:** Lash Lock (+ tissue alt), shadow that drops fallout · **Setup:** Set 1 over-shoulder + macro; **split-face capable** framing.

**Presenter script:**
> "This is the step that makes the whole thing clean. Watch. Same eye, two ways. First, no protection — I'll pack on this shadow like normal... and look: fallout, right onto the under-eye I just perfected. Now I'd have to wipe it, and wiping drags it across my concealer and I'm redoing the whole thing. Sound familiar?
> Now the control step. I hold [Lash Lock / a folded tissue] flush under the lash line, angled to my cheek, like a little shelf. I do the exact same shadow... and watch what it catches. [pause — let the macro show the fallout on the tool.] Nothing reached my under-eye. I lift it away, and there's no cleanup. That's it. That's the difference between 'nice eyes' and 'messy face you have to fix.'
> I use Lash Lock because it's reusable and sits at the right angle, but the *method* is the point — a tissue works in a pinch. Hold, apply, lift. One move."

- **Demonstration:** (1) unprotected side → fallout → smear when wiped; (2) protected side → macro of catch → clean lift. Show the tissue alternative once.
- **Camera:** over-shoulder for the hold; **macro** for the catch (the whole video lives or dies on this shot); split-face if achievable.
- **Close-ups:** tool angle under lash line; fallout landing on tool; clean under-eye reveal.
- **B-roll:** slow-mo fallout drop; tool lift-away.
- **On-screen text:** "STEP 3 · CONTROL" · "Watch what it catches" · "No cleanup".
- **Graphic callouts:** arrow to caught fallout.
- **Editing:** do NOT cut during the catch reveal — one continuous take = trust. Hold the clean reveal 2s.
- **Music/sound:** drop music out just before the catch; let silence + real sound sell it; music back on the reveal.
- **Continuity:** same shadow/lid as Lesson 5.
- **Safety/claim:** ⚠️CLAIM-GATE — spoken claim limited to "catches fallout / no cleanup," and **only publish once product tests #1–2 pass.** Include the honest tissue alternative. No "guaranteed/zero fallout" language.
- **Thumbnail:** "Watch what this catches". **Export:** clean+captioned + a standalone social cut (this is also organic gold). **QC:** catch is uncut & legible; honest alt shown; no over-claim; macro in focus.
