## A. Beauty-education competitors (Australia)

**Verified landscape (searched):** The Australian online makeup-education market is dominated by *long, expensive certificate courses* — Australian Beauty School, Academy of Makeup, IMUA, Online Courses Australia, Kryolan. Typical shape: **$2,500–$5,000**, 100–200+ hours, "become a professional MUA," delivered via LMS with kits posted out, payment plans $30–$50/week. Source URLs in `_sources.md`.

| Layer | Who serves it | Price | Promise | Gap for Kat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Certificate / career | ABS, Academy of Makeup, IMUA | $2,500–$5,000 | "Become a pro MUA" | Too big, slow, career-focused. Ignores the hobbyist who just wants *her own* eyes to look good. |
| Free social tutorials | Thousands of creators (TikTok/IG/YT) | $0 | "Watch me do a look" | Free, unstructured, not repeatable, no outcome, no support. The customer's #1 objection ("it's all free online") lives *here*. |
| **Focused paid micro-class ($9–$79)** | **[ASSUMPTION] thin/absent in AU beauty** — common in US via Stan/Kajabi creators, rare & unbranded locally | — | — | **← Kat's white space.** Small, specific, outcome-named, cheap enough to be an impulse buy, premium enough to trust. |

**Per-competitor record (representative, not exhaustive):**

- **Australian Beauty School** — Audience: career-changers. Offer: online certificates. Price: ~$2,500–$5,000, weekly plans. Promise: certification + employability. Format: LMS + posted kit. Strongest angle: accreditation & tutor access 6 days/wk. Weakness: expensive, slow, intimidating for a hobbyist. Objection it triggers: "too much commitment." Trust signals: accreditation, testimonials, physical kit. Funnel: SEO → long sales page → enrol/finance. **Kat's edge:** speed-to-result, single specific outcome, price accessibility.
- **Academy of Makeup / IMUA** — similar shape; strong artist branding. Weakness: same career-scale positioning. **Kat's edge:** hobbyist + "on camera / in AU heat" angle they don't own.
- **Free creators (aggregate)** — Strongest angle: volume, personality, trend-speed. Weakness: no structure, no completion, no support, no repeatable *system*. **Kat's edge:** a named method + downloadable tools + a completion path.

## B. Physical beauty-tool competitors (reusable eye shields)

**Verified (searched):** Category exists and is proven. Leaders are **ShadowMoon** (reusable silicone, launched 2020, "catch fallout / winged-liner stencil / freeze to de-puff", washes with soap & water), **J.Cat Eye Makeup Guard**, **PxLook Shadow Shields**, **Frends Beauty Fallout Protector**. Disposable adhesive shields also compete.

| Attribute | Category norm | Complaint patterns [ASSUMPTION, verify in reviews] | Lash Lock opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Material | Silicone (reusable) or paper/adhesive (disposable) | Cheap silicone "feels gimmicky"; adhesive tugs delicate skin | Own a *premium, MUA-endorsed* feel; verified material story |
| Claims | "No fallout / perfect wings / de-puff" | Over-claims; results depend heavily on technique | **Under-claim, over-demonstrate.** Pair tool with *the method* — the education makes it work |
| Positioning | Sold as a gadget | Gadget = low trust, one-time buy | Sold as *"the tool a working MUA actually reaches for"* + reorder via consumables/education |
| Demo style | Quick "look no fallout" clip | Staged, hard to believe | Split-face, real-time, honest — including where it *doesn't* help |
| Channel | Amazon / DTC / subscription box | Commoditised, price war | Bundle with mini-class = escape the price war |

**Strategic read:** The tool category is *validated* (people buy these) but *undifferentiated and trust-poor*. Lash Lock's moat is **not** the plastic — it's **Kat's credibility + the method that makes the tool actually deliver.** The tool sells the class; the class sells the tool.

## C. Content competitors (short-form beauty) — pattern audit

[ASSUMPTION — patterns from general category knowledge + searches; owner to validate against a live scroll of 15–20 AU beauty creators before finalising Brand Bible.]

**Winning conventions (do these):**
- First frame = the *problem or result*, not a face saying hi.
- Split-screen / side-by-side proof.
- Fast, close-up demonstration of one technique.
- Big, high-contrast on-screen text; caption-first (sound-off) design.
- One idea per video.

**Overused / fatigued (avoid or subvert):**
- "Get ready with me" with no teaching payoff.
- "Hi guys, so today…" intros.
- Gold-glitter luxury B-roll with no function.
- "This changed my life" over-claims on gadgets.
- 12-product routines that overwhelm beginners.

**Repeated viewer questions (from category comment patterns):** "What's that tool?" · "Does it actually work or is it staged?" · "Will this work on mature/hooded/oily eyes?" · "How do I stop fallout without redoing my concealer?" · "Link?" — these become content and FAQ fuel.

## E. White-space position (defendable)

> **"The Australian working makeup artist who teaches a clean, repeatable eye-makeup *method* — the un-glamorous professional steps that make everyday makeup look good in person, on camera, and in real Australian heat — and gives you the exact tool and checklist to do it yourself."**

Own the word **"clean"** (as in *controlled, no-mess, no-fallout*), not "glam." Own **"method,"** not "look." Own **"in Australian conditions,"** which the big schools and US tool brands cannot credibly claim.

## F. "100% better" — defined as measurable, not boastful

Per brief §5, no objective-superiority claims. Instead, the system commits to being *materially better* on these measurable axes vs category norms:

1. **Faster comprehension** — one idea per video; a named 5-step method vs sprawling tutorials.
2. **Stronger visual proof** — split-face, real-time, sound-off-legible.
3. **More specific outcomes** — "cleaner under-eyes after eyeshadow," not "look prettier."
4. **More honest claims** — every tool claim verified before publishing (§ product checklist).
5. **Better product continuity** — tool ↔ class reinforce, without every video being an ad.
6. **More useful downloads** — checklists that make the method repeatable off-screen.
7. **Better testing discipline** — one-variable testing, internal baselines (no fake benchmarks).
8. **Less robotic AI** — real footage for all hands/eye/product interaction; AI only for B-roll/polish.
