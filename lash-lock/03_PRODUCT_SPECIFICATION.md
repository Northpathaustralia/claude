# PART 1 — ROUND 4: FINAL HERO PRODUCT

## 1.1 Identity

- **Product name:** Lash Lock™ (hero tool) · sold as the **Lash Lock Duo Kit — Clean Eye System**
- **Brand:** Katch Beauty (provisional, pending clearance)
- **Category:** Reusable eye-makeup application shield (passive beauty tool; not a cosmetic)
- **Purpose:** Catch eyeshadow/glitter fallout and block mascara/liner transfer, so finished base makeup stays clean — while doubling as a liner straight-edge and mascara guard.

## 1.2 Product description (customer-facing draft)

> Lash Lock is a featherlight silicone shield that hugs the curve under your eye and catches everything your eyeshadow drops — glitter, shimmer, pigment — before it ruins your base. Flip it, and the precision wing edge guides razor-sharp liner and keeps mascara off your skin. Rinse, dry, done. Two shields, a hygienic case, a fallout brush and pro technique cards in every kit.

## 1.3 User instructions (v1 draft — refine after fit panel)

1. **Seat it:** Hold the grip tab. Place the feather edge just under your lower lash line, following the curve of your eye. Light press — the shield hugs and stays. *(If doing a full glam look, seat before shadow; over finished concealer it lifts away without a mark.)*
2. **Make the mess:** Apply shadow, glitter, pigment. Fallout lands in the catch channel, not on your face.
3. **Lift away:** Peel from the outer corner. Tap the fallout into the bin.
4. **Wing & lash:** Use the straight wing edge as a liner guide; hold the shield behind lashes when applying mascara to the bottom or top lashes.
5. **Clean:** Warm water + mild soap, air dry, back in the case. Pros: 70% IPA wipe between clients *(claim pending material confirmation — RRV)*.

# PART 2 — ROUND 10: MANUFACTURING & PROTOTYPING GUIDE (BEGINNER-FRIENDLY)

## 2.1 The path from sketch to product

**Step 1 — Sketches (Week 1, $0).** Hand-sketch the five diagram views (1.6). Photograph them. These + the spec table are enough for a designer conversation.

**Step 2 — Product specification (done).** This document, Part 1. Give it whole to designers/manufacturers.

**Step 3 — 3D modelling ($150–500 EST).** A freelance industrial designer (Upwork/Fiverr/local — search "industrial designer silicone consumer product") converts the spec into a CAD file (STEP + STL). Ask for: 3 durometer-agnostic geometry variants (width 72/78/84 mm), draft angles for moulding, and a negative mould model for 3D printing. Gold Coast/Brisbane option: university industrial-design students or local product studios for the same job. **RRV quotes.**

**Step 4 — 3D printing the mould ($30–80/iteration EST).** Print the two-part negative mould in resin (local print bureau, library makerspace, or online service). The mould, not the shield, gets printed — silicone itself can't be usefully 3D printed at this budget.

**Step 5 — Silicone prototyping at the kitchen bench ($60–120 EST).** Buy platinum-cure silicone (Shore A 20/30/40 sample kit — art/special-effects suppliers e.g. Barnes Australia stock these; **ASSUMED availability, RRV**), mix, degas as well as possible (vibration/tapping), pour, cure, demould. Expect ugly first pulls; iterate. Safety note: platinum-cure two-part silicone is skin-safe when cured, but *prototypes are for fit testing, not resale*.

**Step 6 — Supplier samples ($100–300 EST).** In parallel, send spec + STEP file to 3–5 silicone OEMs (Alibaba "custom silicone beauty tool", plus 1–2 AU/NZ mouldshops for comparison). Ask each for material certs and an existing similar product sample first (costs ~$10–30 shipped) to judge quality before any tooling talk. **VERIFIED pattern:** silicone OEMs advertise low-MOQ custom work from ~100–500 units with mould costs typically borne by the customer and mould ownership transferable ([NEWTOP](https://www.newtopsilicone.com/custom-service/), [ZSR](https://consumersiliconeproducts.com/custom-silicone-products), [HB Silicone pricing guide](https://www.hbsilicone.com/how-much-does-custom-silicone-molding-cost-a-complete-oem-pricing-guide-for-silicone-products/)).
**Compression moulding is the recommended process** — lower tooling cost than LSR injection, suited to low volumes and simple single-material parts (**VERIFIED** as industry guidance, same sources). **ESTIMATED tooling: US$500–2,500 for a small multi-cavity compression mould; unit price US$0.30–1.50 at 500–2,000 units. Real quotes RRV.**

**Step 7 — Tooling & pilot run (only after GO).** Pay tooling; demand: mould ownership in your name in writing, T1 samples (first shots) approved against the golden sample before mass production, 300–500 unit pilot run.

**Step 8 — Packaging (parallel).** Stock clamshell case + custom printed sleeve/box (digital print, low MOQ 250–500; **ESTIMATED $0.80–2.00/unit**). Instruction leaflet + technique cards same printer.

**Step 9 — QC & shipping.** Inspect per checklist (2.5); air courier for ≤500 units (**ESTIMATED $4–8/kg; kit ~90g packed → freight allocation ~$0.60–1.20/unit**); GST + import declarations apply — most consumer goods from China enter AU duty-free under ChAFTA but confirm HS code (**ASSUMED**; customs broker check ⚖️).

**Step 10 — Product testing.** Fit panel (10+ faces), 30-wash durability cycle, IPA-wipe material test, drop/tear test, 2-week daily-use trial by Kat + 3 MUAs. Log everything.

## 2.4 Supplier questionnaire (score each 1–5)

1. Years producing skin-contact silicone products? Examples?
2. Material: platinum or peroxide cure? Certificates supplied per batch?
3. Mould ownership in writing? Cost to transfer mould out?
4. MOQ and unit price breaks; T1 sample policy and cost?
5. Tolerances achievable on a 0.5 mm feather edge?
6. QC process: in-line checks, final AQL standard, photos before ship?
7. Do you sign an NNN/NDA-style agreement (non-disclosure, non-use, non-circumvention)?
8. Lead times: tooling, samples, production, peak-season variance?
9. Payment terms (30/70 vs 50/50; escrow/trade-assurance available)?
10. References or store links of brands you supply?

## 2.5 Checklists & scorecard

**Sample inspection checklist:** dimensions vs drawing (±0.15 mm body, feather edge intact 360°); durometer reading; no tears/flash/voids/inclusions; finish zones correct; odour test (strong smell = cure problem); colour match; tab strength (pull test); channel holds loose powder when tilted 45°; survives 30 wash cycles + 20 IPA wipes without clouding/tack (log); certs received and batch-matched.

**Supplier comparison scorecard (weight in brackets):** quality of sample (25) · certs & compliance (20) · price at 500 units (15) · tooling cost/ownership terms (15) · communication speed & clarity (10) · MOQ flexibility (10) · references (5). Score 3+ suppliers before choosing.

**Production QC checklist (per batch):** golden-sample comparison on 32-unit random draw (AQL 2.5-ish; ⚖️/RRV exact standard); dimension spot-check 8 units; wash + IPA spot-test 4 units; packaging integrity; leaflet/warning presence; carton drop test; photos archived.

## 2.6 Risk reduction summary

| Risk | Reduction |
|---|---|
| Poor quality | Sample-first ordering; golden sample sign-off; T1 approval gate; AQL inspection; small pilot run |
| Large MOQs | Compression moulding; suppliers advertising 100–500 MOQ; pilot 300–500 units; never exceed 6 weeks demand cover |
| Supplier fraud | Trade assurance/escrow payments; video call + live factory tour; sample before deposit; two suppliers qualified so no hostage situation |
| Product copying | NNN agreement; mould ownership; AU registered design filed pre-reveal; brand/system moat (see Round 19 W2) |
| Wrong materials | Batch certs as PO condition; independent lab verification of first batch (**ESTIMATED $300–800**; ⚖️/RRV) if claims require it |
| Freight surprises | Quote DDP (delivered duty paid) vs FOB both ways; kit weighed/dimensioned early; customs broker sanity check ⚖️ |
