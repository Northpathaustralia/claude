# ROUND 1 — CUSTOMER PROBLEM

## 1.1 The exact problem

When eye makeup is applied — especially shimmer, glitter, deeply pigmented shadow, or mascara — loose particles and wet product fall or transfer onto the under-eye and upper cheek. This is called **fallout** (powder/glitter) and **transfer/smudge** (mascara, liner). The result:

1. Fallout lands on finished base makeup (foundation/concealer) and cannot be brushed away cleanly — removing it disturbs the base underneath.
2. The user must either redo the under-eye area, or accept a muddied, "dirty" under-eye look.
3. Mascara wands and liner slips stamp product onto the lid or under-eye, forcing spot-cleanup.

**VERIFIED** — this is a mainstream, widely documented problem. Beauty publishers maintain evergreen how-to content on preventing fallout ([Makeup.com by L'Oréal](https://www.makeup.com/makeup-tutorials/eyes/how-to-apply-glitter-eyeshadow-without-fallout), [Bodyography](https://www.bodyography.com/blogs/news/how-to-apply-glitter-eyeshadow-without-fallout)), and multiple commercial products exist solely to solve it (see Round 2).

## 1.2 Severity and frequency

- **Frequency:** occurs to some degree on most shadow applications; severe with shimmers, glitters, and deep pigments. For daily makeup wearers this is a multiple-times-per-week annoyance; for glam/event makeup it is near-universal. **ESTIMATED** (from the volume of workaround content and products; exact incidence RRV).
- **Severity:** moderate — it costs **time** (2–10 minutes of cleanup/redo), **product** (wasted concealer/foundation), and **result quality** (muddy under-eye in photos). It is not a health or safety problem. This matters: it is a *convenience/perfection* problem, which supports an impulse-priced accessory (< AUD $50), not a high-ticket product. **ESTIMATED**.

## 1.3 How customers solve it today

| Current solution | Cost | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Tissue/business card held under eye | ~$0 | Occupies one hand; slips; can't do detail work one-handed; crushes lashes |
| Loose translucent powder "baking" under eye, brushed off after | ~$0 (uses owned product) | Extra steps; drying; doesn't work under mascara; doesn't stop glitter adhesion |
| "Eyes first, base second" application order | $0 | Forces workflow change most users dislike; doesn't help touch-ups over finished base |
| Sticky tape to lift glitter | ~$0 | Irritates skin; lifts base makeup; feels wrong near eyes |
| Disposable adhesive shields (Shadow Shields, AOA Shadow Armor) | ~US$10–12 per 30 (**VERIFIED**, [Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.com/Shadow-Shields-Michelle-Villanueva-smudge-free/dp/B00UCEMBXI)) | Ongoing cost per application; adhesive on delicate under-eye skin; single position, "can't be easily adjusted" (**VERIFIED** complaint, [Influenster reviews](https://www.influenster.com/reviews/shadow-shields-the-original-eye-shadow-makeup-application-shields-14-count)); waste |
| Reusable silicone guards (ShadowMoon, J.Cat BR57, generics) | ~US$4–15 (**VERIFIED**, [ShadowMoon](https://shadowmoonbeauty.com/products/shadowmoon), [J.Cat](https://jcatbeauty.com/products/eye-makeup-guard)) | Mostly single flat paddles; generic fit; must be held in place; low-brand, low-instruction products; hygiene between uses unaddressed |
| Fan brush + makeup remover pen cleanup after the fact | $10–30 | Fixes symptom, not cause; still disturbs base |

**VERIFIED** — sources above plus [Makeup and Beauty Blog tissue-shield tip](https://makeupandbeautyblog.com/makeup-tipshow-to/quick-makeup-tip-fight-glitter-eyeshadow-fallout-by-shielding-your-under-eye-area-with-a-tissue/).

## 1.4 Professionals vs home users

- **Professional makeup artists (MUAs):** face the problem *on clients*, many times per day. Their constraints differ: hygiene between clients is non-negotiable; speed matters (redoing under-eye costs booking time); they already buy disposable shields from pro suppliers (Frends Beauty sells a silicone Eye Makeup Fallout Protector — **VERIFIED**, [Frends Beauty](https://frendsbeauty.com/products/eye-makeup-fallout-protector)). A pro tool must be sanitisable (70% IPA), fast to position, and work on many face shapes. **VERIFIED/ESTIMATED** — pro product existence verified; workflow detail is informed by Kat's professional experience and is **RRV** with a wider MUA sample.
- **Home users:** face it on themselves, in a mirror, often one-handed. They need a tool that self-locates and stays put without being held, or is trivially held with the non-dominant hand. They care about not creasing concealer and not crushing lashes.

**Kat is the bridge:** she experiences the pro version of the problem and can credibly demonstrate the consumer version. This dual-lens is a genuine content and product-development advantage.

## 1.5 Is the problem strong enough to support a commercial product?

**Yes, with honest caveats:**

- **For:** multiple companies already sell dedicated solutions; Shadow Shields achieved national US media (E! News, TODAY, Oprah Magazine — **VERIFIED** via [VERO Professionals product page](https://veroprofessionals.com/products/shadow-shields)); the problem is visually demonstrable in 5 seconds of video — ideal for short-form content; The Quick Flick proved an Australian single-SKU eye-tool brand can reach $10M and 1,500+ stores (**VERIFIED**, [Yahoo Lifestyle](https://au.lifestyle.yahoo.com/quick-flick-beauty-idea-made-iris-smit-a-multimillionaire-050330227.html), [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/the-quick-flick-breakdown)).
- **Against:** the $0 workarounds (tissue, powder) are "good enough" for many; existing dedicated products are cheap; nobody appears to have built a *premium brand* on this problem — which is either white space or a signal demand is shallow. **This is the single most important question validation must answer** (Round 5). **RRV**.

**Round 1 conclusion:** Real, frequent, visually demonstrable problem with existing paid demand but no premium brand leader. Proceed to market review.

## 5.2 Decision rules

- **GO** (to Budget 3 launch spend): Tests 1, 2, 3 pass AND (6 or 7 passes). Order first production run (300–500 units max).
- **IMPROVE**: Tests 1 & 3 pass but 2 fails → iterate geometry (reprint mould, ~$50/iteration) and retest. Do not touch budget 3.
- **PIVOT**: Test 1 passes but 2 & 7 fail after two iterations → the pain is real but this tool isn't the answer; pivot within category using the same audience (candidates: Concept D pro hygiene system; mascara-guard-first positioning; or a different visible problem surfaced by the audience itself).
- **STOP**: Test 1 fails (problem content gets no traction across 10+ videos on two platforms) AND MUA interviews are lukewarm → shelve the product thesis; keep the audience asset; total loss capped at < AUD $1,000.

**Kat — Founder-Face, Product Expert, Creative Lead** (if she says yes): product design input and testing authority (her word is final on "does this work on a real face"), content star and creator, MUA community liaison, quality standards. Accountable for: product truth and brand credibility.

## 8.3 The hard, honest part — protecting both people

Sole control by James is a legitimate way to start a venture he is funding and risk-managing. It is **not** a substitute for fair, written agreements — especially because the brand's public value will be built substantially on Kat's face, name, skill, and labour. If this is mishandled it can damage both the business and the relationship. Practical measures:

1. **Written role & contribution agreement from day one Kat participates.** Even one page. ⚖️
2. **IP assignment clarity:** content Kat creates, product feedback she gives, and any design input should be assigned to the company in writing, *in exchange for defined consideration* (pay, revenue share, or equity path). ⚖️
3. **Image/likeness licence, not assumption:** the company should *licence* Kat's name/face for marketing, revocable on defined terms if she ever steps back. This protects Kat. ⚖️
4. **Pay her from the first dollar the business can afford it** — contractor invoices (she may already have an ABN as an MUA) or employment. ⚖️ Employment vs contractor classification is a legal question — get advice; superannuation obligations can apply either way.
5. **Performance incentives:** simple revenue-share bonus on sales attributable to content/pro channels she drives (e.g. a % of net sales as a creative royalty) — cleaner than early equity. ⚖️
6. **Future equity options:** pre-agree a milestone-based path (e.g. Kat may acquire/vest up to X% at defined milestones or events), documented by a lawyer with a shareholders' agreement when triggered. ⚖️ Also consider what happens to roles/ownership for both a business failure and a relationship change — uncomfortable, essential. ⚖️
7. **Anniversary framing:** the gift is an *invitation and an option*, never an obligation. Kat may want to be the face, the product brain, an equal partner later — or none of these. The presentation (`09_ANNIVERSARY_PRESENTATION.md`) is written so that "no" or "not yet" are safe answers.

## 8.4 Matters explicitly requiring professional advice ⚖️

Company vs sole-trader timing and tax; GST registration; trademark clearance and filing strategy (attorney, not just self-filing); employment vs contractor status for Kat + super; product liability insurance scope; consumer-law compliance of marketing claims; shareholders'/option agreements; what "skin-safe/hypoallergenic" claims may legally be made about materials.

# ROUND 19 — RED-TEAM ATTACK

Mission: disprove the business. Severity: CRITICAL / HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW.

## W1. The problem might not be painful enough — CRITICAL
**Attack:** Tissue and powder are free and good enough. Fallout is an annoyance, not a bleeding neck. Shadow Shields, despite Oprah-tier media, remains a small niche product 10+ years on — maybe that *is* the market's honest size.
**Consequence:** No repeatable demand; dead inventory; wasted year.
**Correction:** This is exactly what gates 1, 3, 7 test with ~$0 at risk. The STOP rule exists for this. Additionally: position the product around the *moment of highest pain* (glam/event/glitter looks, MUA client work) not everyday neutral looks.
**Strategy update:** Launch content targets glam/occasion makeup and MUA workflows first; everyday use is the upsell story, not the hook.

## W2. Copyability — CRITICAL
**Attack:** Any factory can copy a silicone crescent in 30 days. If the demo goes viral, AliExpress/Temu listings will undercut at $4 within a quarter. No patent protects the core idea (shields exist; prior art is everywhere).
**Consequence:** Price collapse of the generic version of the idea; ad-free copycats ride the created demand.
**Correction:** Accept that the *shape* will be copied. Build what can't be: (a) AU **registered design** on the specific geometry filed before reveal (cheap, imperfect, real deterrent for AU retail); (b) the brand + Kat's credibility + education content; (c) the *kit/system* (case, brush, hygiene protocol, technique library) which raises the copy bar from "a paddle" to "a brand"; (d) speed to community.
**Strategy update:** Never market the paddle; market the system and the artist. File design registration in week 1 post-GO.

## W3. Premium price vs $6 generics — HIGH
**Attack:** Asking AUD $34.95 for silicone that looks (to a scroller) like a $6 Amazon item invites "it's a piece of silicone" comments.
**Consequence:** Conversion fails despite views; brand mocked in comments.
**Correction:** (a) The kit contains visible substance (2 engineered shields, case, brush, technique cards); (b) anchor against disposables' recurring cost ("a year of Shadow Shields costs more"); (c) unboxing-grade packaging; (d) a $19.95 single-shield entry SKU caps the objection.
**Strategy update:** Add entry SKU; build the cost-per-use comparison into the product page and one hero video.

## W4. Fit across faces — HIGH
**Attack:** One geometry cannot self-seat on deep-set, hooded, mature, and high-cheekbone faces alike. If it slips on 40% of buyers, returns and bad reviews follow.
**Consequence:** Review-score death spiral; the core "stays put hands-free" claim collapses.
**Correction:** Prototype gate 2 requires ≥7/10 real faces seating success *including varied ages and eye shapes*; if impossible, ship in two sizes (S/M split like Quick Flick's four sizes — precedent verified) or reposition the claim to "featherlight hold" (held, but effortless).
**Strategy update:** Fit-testing panel is a named task in the 90-day plan; claims are written only after gate 2 data.

## W5. Organic reach assumption — HIGH
**Attack:** "Post good content, get customers free" is survivorship bias. Most beauty accounts plateau at triple-digit views. Scenarios 3–4 quietly assume virality.
**Consequence:** Beautiful brand, no traffic, morale collapse by month 3.
**Correction:** (a) Scenario maths in `05_FINANCIAL_MODEL.md` are built bottom-up from posting volume × conservative view/conversion rates, and scenarios 3–4 are explicitly labelled as requiring outlier outcomes; (b) creator seeding (20 kits) diversifies reach beyond owned accounts; (c) budget 3 reserves a small paid-boost test line ($300) to price real CAC early.
**Strategy update:** KPI dashboard tracks views→click→email→sale conversion weekly; if organic CAC-equivalent exceeds $25/sale in founder-hours, revisit channel mix at day 60.

## W6. Hygiene claims risk — MEDIUM
**Attack:** "Hygienic" and "sanitisable" are quasi-health claims. Unsubstantiated antimicrobial marketing breaches consumer law; silicone can degrade with repeated IPA exposure.
**Consequence:** ACCC complaints; product returns; pro-community credibility loss.
**Correction:** Claims limited to: "wash with soap and water; suitable for 70% IPA wipe-down [pending material verification with supplier]" — no "antibacterial/antimicrobial" language ever. Material certs (platinum-cure, skin-contact grade) required from supplier before any claim. ⚖️
**Strategy update:** Claims register added to QC checklist; supplier cert is a purchase-order condition.

## W7. Anniversary-gift structure risk — MEDIUM (human), HIGH (if ignored)
**Attack:** A business gifted to Kat, where James owns 100% and Kat is the unpaid face, can read as James gifting *himself* a business staffed by Kat. If the business succeeds on her face and she owns nothing, resentment is structural.
**Consequence:** Relationship strain; brand's public face disengages; the venture's core asset walks.
**Correction:** Round 8 measures adopted: written agreement, likeness licence, pay from first affordable dollar, royalty/incentive, milestone equity path, explicit no-pressure invitation. The presentation is written as an offer of partnership in stages, not a role assignment.
**Strategy update:** The Round 8 protections are non-optional pre-conditions of launch, equal in status to the financial gates.

## W8. Single-product dependence — MEDIUM
**Attack:** One hero SKU = one point of failure (a bad batch, a platform ban, a copycat undercut).
**Consequence:** Fragile revenue; no basket growth.
**Correction:** Ecosystem path already defined (Pro Kit at launch; strips/consumables V2; adjacent shields V3); bundles raise AOV day one.
**Strategy update:** None needed beyond existing roadmap.

## W9. Margin erosion by shipping and fees — MEDIUM
**Attack:** AUD $34.95 AOV with $9–12 domestic parcel cost destroys margin if free shipping is promised.
**Consequence:** Contribution margin per order halves.
**Correction:** Charge shipping below $50; bundle-first merchandising pushes AOV to $45–55; flat-rate satchel products (kit is light/small) keep cost ~$6–10. **ESTIMATED**; AusPost quotes RRV.
**Strategy update:** AOV, not conversion, is the #2 KPI after email capture.

## W10. Trademark/handle availability — MEDIUM
**Attack:** "Katch Beauty" or "Lash Lock" may be taken or confusingly similar to existing marks (multiple "lash" brands exist worldwide).
**Consequence:** Forced rebrand after brand equity exists; legal letters.
**Correction:** Desk-check before any spend (free: IP Australia search, ASIC name search, social handles); TM Headstart Part 1 ($200/class, **VERIFIED**) before Budget 3; attorney check before tooling. Backup names ranked in `04_BRAND_GUIDE.md`. ⚖️
**Strategy update:** Naming is provisional until clearance; nothing is printed/tooled with the name before Headstart response.

## W11. Regulatory/product safety — LOW-MEDIUM
**Attack:** A tool used near the eye could scratch a cornea in misuse; a defect could injure.
**Consequence:** Liability claim; recall.
**Correction:** It is a passive external tool (not a cosmetic, not therapeutic) — general ACL product-safety duty applies (**ASSUMED** classification; ⚖️ confirm). Rounded edges ≥0.5mm, no rigid inserts near the eye edge, batch QC per checklist, product liability insurance before first sale (**ESTIMATED** AUD $500–1,200/yr; quotes RRV), clear usage instructions and warnings.
**Strategy update:** Insurance and instruction-leaflet are launch blockers, listed in the 90-day plan.

## W12. Founder bandwidth — LOW-MEDIUM
**Attack:** James has a career; Kat has clients. Content cadence (5+/week) is a part-time job.
**Consequence:** Cadence collapses at week 6; algorithm forgets the account.
**Correction:** Batch filming (one 2–3h session/week = 6–8 posts), Higgsfield-assisted b-roll/hooks, and a content library buffer of 2 weeks minimum before launch.
**Strategy update:** Buffer requirement added to launch checklist.

**Red-team summary:** No weakness is fatal *given the gates*. Two weaknesses (W1 demand-depth, W2 copyability) are existential and cannot be engineered away — only tested cheaply and out-positioned. The plan survives on the strict condition that spending follows evidence.
