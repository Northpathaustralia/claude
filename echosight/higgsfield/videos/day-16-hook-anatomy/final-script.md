# Final script — Day 16: Anatomy of a 0.8-second hook

Total 36s · VO pace ~3.8 words/sec · ON-SCREEN = burned text overlay

| Time | VO (spoken) | ON-SCREEN | Visual |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | A working hook does three jobs in under one second. Miss one, the scroll wins. | 3 jobs. 0.8 seconds. | Maya holds up three fingers close to lens, sharp focus on hand, face soft behind |
| 3–11s | Job one: something moves. Not a logo fading in — a human, an object, a frame change. Motion is the only thing peripheral vision respects. | ① something MOVES | Maya shoves a coffee mug across the desk; it slides through frame |
| 11–19s | Job two: a claim lands. On screen, not just spoken — half your viewers have sound off. Six words or fewer, readable at arm’s length. | ② a CLAIM lands (≤6 words) | Maya holds up a hand-written card reading “your back isn’t tight. it’s this.” |
| 19–27s | Job three: a face, if you have one. Eyes with intent, mid-expression — not composed and waiting. We are wired to check faces first. | ③ a FACE with intent | Maya snaps from neutral waiting-face to animated mid-sentence expression to show the difference |
| 27–36s | Move, claim, face — inside zero point eight. Save this and check your last three hooks against it. The checklist version’s on screen now. | ✓ move ✓ claim ✓ face — by 0:00.8 | Maya leans back, taps the on-screen space beside her as if pinning the checklist there |

**VO delivery:** Crisp. The three jobs are the spine — hit each word: MOVE, CLAIM, FACE.
