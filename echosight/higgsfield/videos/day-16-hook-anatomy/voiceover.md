# Voiceover — Day 16: Anatomy of a 0.8-second hook

Record dry (phone in quiet room, 20cm from mouth) or generate. Delivery: Crisp. The three jobs are the spine — hit each word: MOVE, CLAIM, FACE.

**[0–3s]** A working hook does three jobs in under one second. Miss one, the scroll wins.

**[3–11s]** Job one: something moves. Not a logo fading in — a human, an object, a frame change. Motion is the only thing peripheral vision respects.

**[11–19s]** Job two: a claim lands. On screen, not just spoken — half your viewers have sound off. Six words or fewer, readable at arm’s length.

**[19–27s]** Job three: a face, if you have one. Eyes with intent, mid-expression — not composed and waiting. We are wired to check faces first.

**[27–36s]** Move, claim, face — inside zero point eight. Save this and check your last three hooks against it. The checklist version’s on screen now.

Timing check: read aloud at ~3.8 words/sec; each line must fit its window with ≥0.3s air. If a line runs long, cut words — never speed the read.
