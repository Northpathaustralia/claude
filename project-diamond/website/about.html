We started with a fight about a candle.

Every gift for "someone who does too much" is a candle. We wanted to give something that gave back an hour — not an object, a practice.

So we spent a year with a Tasmanian distiller, a cosmetic chemist, and a lot of bathwater, building the wind-down ritual we couldn't buy: honest about the science, uncompromising about the sensory, beautiful enough to keep.

Evenfall is made in Melbourne, scented in Tasmania, and used at roughly 9:14pm.

What a bath can and can't do

We won't tell you magnesium soaks through skin and fixes your sleep — the science there is genuinely unsettled, and brands that pretend otherwise are selling you certainty they don't have.

Here's what is well-established: a warm bath before bed helps your body do what it's designed to do — warm, then cool, then rest. Twenty screen-free minutes does the rest. Evenfall exists to make that hour so good you'll actually keep it.

The ritual

01 — Draw. Run the bath warm, not hot. Thirty-eight degrees is the sweet spot: warm enough to raise your core temperature so it can fall afterwards, which is the drop your body reads as "time to rest".

02 — Pour. One scoop. Watch the petals surface. The phone stays outside the door — this is the hard step, and the one that matters most.

03 — Descend. Twenty minutes. A book if you like. That's the whole job. No tub? Half a scoop in a basin of warm water — the foot-soak protocol card is in every jar.
