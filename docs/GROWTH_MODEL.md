# GROWTH MODEL — FROM LABEL TO ECOSYSTEM

Fixes RED_TEAM_AUDIT findings #22 (revenue model), #25 (retention), #26 (virality),
#21 (community). Builds the 5/10-year arc, the non-clothing revenue, the loyalty loops and
the exit thesis. **All figures AUD, scenario-based, assumption-driven — not guarantees.**
Extends `REVENUE_FORECAST.md` (year 1) and `UNIT_ECONOMICS.md` (per-order math).

---

## 1. The thesis in one paragraph

A premium apparel brand is worth far more than its clothing margin when it owns a
*community* and a *founder story*. TIDESTATE's plan: use honest, build-in-public content to
acquire cheaply, use climate-built product to retain, then layer ecosystem revenue
(membership, events, accessories, media) that compounds LTV without proportional CAC. The
clothing is the front door; the community is the business.

## 2. Five-year revenue arc (three scenarios)

Anchored to Year-1 "Expected" (~$520k, `REVENUE_FORECAST.md`). Growth rates assume
disciplined reinvestment, 2–4 drops/quarter, wholesale from Y2, international from Y2–3, and
ecosystem revenue from Y2. **Assumptions, not promises.**

| Year | Conservative | Expected | Breakout | Key unlocks (Expected) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $285k | $520k | $880k | DTC only, 5-product range → 2 drops |
| 2 | $620k | $1.4m | $2.6m | Wholesale (Y2 H2), membership launches, range to ~15 SKUs |
| 3 | $1.1m | $3.2m | $6.5m | International (NZ→US), accessories line, first flagship pop-up |
| 4 | $1.8m | $5.6m | $12m | US warehouse (cf. Babyboo), events P&L, licensing tests |
| 5 | $2.6m | $8.5m | $20m+ | Multi-category lifestyle brand, retail/wholesale/DTC mix |

**Blended contribution margin** trends 47% (Y1, Model 1) → 53% (Model 2) → 55–58% (scale,
Model 3 + ecosystem). Net margin realistically 8–15% while reinvesting for growth; higher
only if growth is deliberately slowed.

### Ten-year posture (directional, not forecast)

Two honest end-states by Y10: (a) **profitable independent** — $15–30m revenue, 15–20% net,
a genuine Australian icon paying the founder well; or (b) **acquisition** — a strategic or
PE buyer at 1.5–3× revenue for a brand with community + margin + IP. Both are wins; (a) is
the default plan, (b) is optionality, never the strategy. No decision here is made to
"dress up for sale."

## 3. Revenue engines (diversify beyond the tee)

Ranked by *how well they fit the brand and margin*, not novelty. Only add an engine when the
prior one is profitable — no engine before its time.

| Tier | Engine | When | Why it fits | Margin note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | **Apparel drops** | Now | The front door | 74–81% GM |
| Core | **Accessories** (bags, bottles, caps, socks) | Y1 H2 | High-margin, giftable, AOV lift | 70–85% GM |
| Retention | **First Light Club membership** | Y2 | Recurring revenue + belonging | ~90% GM on fee |
| Community | **Events** (run/coffee/breakfast, ticketed later) | Y1→Y2 | Content + acquisition + belonging | Break-even→small profit; halo is the point |
| Channel | **Wholesale** | Y2 | Reach + legitimacy (cf. Front Runner→Rebel) | 45–55% GM, high volume |
| Channel | **International DTC** | Y2–3 | AU market is a beachhead, not the ceiling | Watch freight/duty |
| Media | **The Build** (podcast/YouTube/journal → sponsors) | Y1→ | Founder brand monetises the audience directly | High GM, low cash |
| Media | **Coffee-table book / seasonal zine** | Y3 | Object of value, brand-defining, giftable | 50%+ GM, halo |
| Extend | **Lifestyle** (coffee, fragrance, home) — *licensed/partnered* | Y4+ | Only via partners; never dilute the core | Licensing royalty |

**Discipline rule:** every non-apparel engine must (a) fit the climate-built / Gold Coast /
honest-founder story, (b) beat 60% contribution or serve acquisition, and (c) not distract
the core range. Kill anything that becomes a "brand tax" instead of a brand asset. (No
supplements, no crypto, no drop-shipped tat — they'd torch the positioning.)

## 4. Retention system (fixes the 69)

LTV is the whole game. Layered loops:

- **Free first exchange + garment passport** (live in prototype) → confidence to buy again.
- **First Light Club tiers:**
  | Tier | Entry | Perks |
  |---|---|---|
  | List | free (email) | 48h early access, votes, journal |
  | Club | 2 orders / $300 LTV | numbered patch, event invites first, early restocks |
  | Core | $800 LTV / member fee (Y2) | annual credit, VIP tailoring/repair, private dinners, name in the book |
- **365-day repair program** (Y2): send it back, we fix it — the anti-throwaway signal that
  *is* the climate-built ethos, and a retention/PR asset competitors won't copy.
- **Post-purchase depth:** founder thank-you video, care guide, wear-check at 30 days,
  restock alerts on requested sizes. (Flows exist in `EMAIL_AND_SMS.md`; automate via Klaviyo.)
- **Birthday gift** (sticker pack / early pick, not a discount) — delight, not margin leak.

Target metrics: 60-day repeat ≥ 12% → 25% by Y2; email/SMS revenue share 25% → 35%;
LTV:CAC ≥ 3:1 sustained.

## 5. Virality & referral (fixes the 62)

Honesty *is* the share mechanic — but engineer the loops:

- **Referral:** give a mate first-exchange-free + $15, get $15 (margin-safe within the
  discount allowance). Built into `EMAIL_AND_SMS.md` Flow 13.
- **Numbered editions** (e.g. orange cap /150) → owners post the number → scarcity proof
  without fake scarcity.
- **"Ask us anything about our costs"** content → screenshots of the P&L spread → earned
  reach. The competitor won't do this; it's the wedge.
- **Event content** → every First Light Club event is a content engine and a
  bring-a-friend loop.
- **Garment passport QR** → scannable in the wild → curiosity → site.
- **Share-worthy packaging** (§ PACKAGING doc TODO) → unboxing UGC.

## 6. Community expansion (fixes the 74)

**First Light Club** is the flywheel. Sequence: Gold Coast (Y1, monthly) → Brisbane +
Sunshine Coast (Y2) → Sydney + Melbourne (Y2–3) → template so any city's top customer can
host. Formats: sunrise walk + coffee, business breakfast, training session, beach clean-up,
photography walk, founder Q&A, VIP dinner. Each city's launch = a drop moment. The community
is the moat no ad budget can buy.

## 7. Operating build-out (what scales behind revenue)

| Stage | Revenue band | Team | Ops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Founder-run | <$500k | James + 1 PT | Self-fulfil → 3PL |
| First hires | $500k–2m | +CX, +content/creator lead | 3PL, Klaviyo automation |
| Team | $2m–5m | +ops/inventory, +marketing, +designer | AU 3PL + US 3PL, forecasting |
| Company | $5m+ | Dept leads, +wholesale, +retail | Multi-warehouse, ERP, planning |

Cash discipline throughout: inventory bought to *proven* demand, GST ring-fenced, ad spend
capped to trailing revenue, no office/warehouse/celebrity spend before it's earned
(`UNIT_ECONOMICS.md` cost-control laws hold at every stage).

## 8. AI & automation (leverage, not headcount)

Automate the repeatable so the team stays small: CX first-response + macros, review
collection, back-in-stock + abandonment flows, social scheduling, demand/inventory
forecasting from sell-through, product recommendations, LTV/cohort dashboards, ad-creative
variant testing. **Never automate:** the founder's voice, real community, real reviews, or
anything that fakes a human relationship — automating *those* would break the honesty moat.

## 9. Risks (the hostile view, kept honest)

Inventory over-buy (mitigate: buy to proven demand); CAC inflation (mitigate: organic/
founder engine, cap ad spend); founder-dependency (mitigate: document, build team, systemise
the story); trend fade (mitigate: category ownership > hype); copycats (mitigate: community
+ proof + honesty can't be cloned); cash crunch at scale-up (mitigate: supplier terms,
staged buys, optional facility *before* the crunch, never during). None of these are solved
on a spreadsheet — they're managed monthly against actuals.

## 10. The one-line strategy

**Acquire with honesty, retain with product, compound with community — and never ship
anything that would force us to delete a video.**
