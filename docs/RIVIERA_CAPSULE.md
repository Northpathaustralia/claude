# THE RIVIERA SET — CAPSULE STRATEGY, SOURCING & RED TEAM

A dedicated, lower-risk capsule inside TIDESTATE: **matching short-sleeve camp-collar
shirt + tailored short sets**, European/coastal smart-casual, for men. Built to test a
different sourcing model (dropship/private-label, not full custom cut-sew) without
touching the core DROP 001 range or its construction claims.

> **Why this lives inside TIDESTATE, not as a new brand.** "Smart-casual, beach-to-bar,
> Gold Coast climate" is exactly the existing positioning (`BRAND_BLUEPRINT.md`). Splitting
> it into a separate brand would duplicate the audience-building work already underway for
> no reason. What *does* change is the sourcing model and — critically — the marketing
> claims allowed on this specific line (see §5).

## 1. Category audit — who's actually winning here

Desk research (July 2026), matching-set / resort shirt category:

- **The category is dominated by generic, low-brand-equity sellers**: COOFANDY, Floerns,
  URRU, FARVALUE, VIPWOLF, LINENNMAIN, GORGLITTER, Babioboa — mostly Amazon-native, priced
  US$25–50, near-zero brand voice, near-zero repeat-purchase design, and reviewed purely on
  fit/fabric-feel, not story. [Amazon Best Sellers — Men's Short & Top Sets](https://us.amazon.com/Best-Sellers-Men's-Short-Top-Sets/zgbs/fashion/203313532011).
- **No dominant premium/DTC player** occupies this exact niche in Australia the way Front
  Runner owns streetwear. That is the opening: a well-photographed, well-written, honestly
  positioned brand can out-market an entire category of anonymous listings without
  outspending anyone.
- **Trend direction (2026):** away from "loud novelty prints and tourist pieces" toward
  *refined, wearable* resort style — clean whites, soft coastal tones, minimal stripes,
  small understated detail rather than busy tropical print. [Globridge Studio — Best Matching Shirt and Shorts Sets for Summer Vacation](https://globridgestudio.com/blogs/news/best-matching-shirt-and-shorts-sets-for-summer-vacation), [Globridge Studio — Best Beach Vacation Outfits for Men 2026](https://globridgestudio.com/blogs/news/the-best-beach-vacation-outfits-for-men-in-2026).
- **Implication for design:** our existing brand guardrail against "palm trees / tourist
  souvenir" isn't a constraint here — it's *literally where the category is trending
  anyway*. Stripe, micro-check, and solid coastal tones (see §3) are both on-brand and
  on-trend, not a compromise.

## 4. The three honest sourcing tiers

This is the part most "just dropship it" advice skips. Real 2026 landscape, confirmed via
supplier research ([CJdropshipping — Best Dropshipping Clothing Suppliers](https://cjdropshipping.com/blogs/dropshipping-knowledge/Best-Dropshipping-Clothing-Suppliers), [Zendrop — Best Dropshipping Suppliers 2026](https://www.zendrop.com/blog/best-dropshipping-suppliers/)):
**true private-label branding almost always requires a batch buy into your own/a 3PL's
warehouse — factories don't custom-brand single units on demand.** Three real tiers, not
a fantasy "zero-inventory zero-cost" version:

### Tier A — True dropship (generic, unbranded garment)
Per-order fulfilment from a supplier catalogue (CJdropshipping / AppScenic-style), your
brand appears only on the packing slip/insert card, not the product itself. Fastest to
launch, zero inventory risk, **but you cannot claim any construction spec** — you don't
choose the fabric, and quality varies by listing. Shipping from China 1–3 weeks even with
faster "ePacket"-style options unless the supplier holds AU/US stock.

### Tier B — Private-label dropship (branded, held-and-fulfilled)
You commission a small custom-label batch (typically 100–300 units/style minimum) shipped
to the supplier's or your own 3PL warehouse; they still fulfil per-order, but the product
now carries your neck label/hang tag. Middle ground: real brand asset, real (if limited)
QC input via a pre-shipment sample, moderate upfront cash.

### Tier C — Small-batch private manufacture (same model as core TIDESTATE range)
Full custom spec, sampling, QC, and your own 3PL — effectively `UNIT_ECONOMICS.md` Model 2
applied to this range. Best margin and quality control, slowest to launch, highest cash
need. This is where the capsule graduates to *if it sells*.

### Economics comparison (AOV $109 set, AUD, ex-GST)

| | Tier A — True dropship | Tier B — Private-label dropship | Tier C — Small-batch manufacture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit COGS (set) | $28–38 | $22–30 | $17–24 |
| Startup cash | ~$300 (samples only) | ~$4,000–7,000 (100–150 unit batch + 3PL setup) | ~$14,000–20,000 (300+ units, full spec) |
| Fulfilment | Supplier, 1–3 wks | Supplier/3PL, held stock, 2–5 days once landed | Your 3PL, 1–2 days |
| Gross margin | 45–55% | 55–65% | 68–75% |
| Brand control | None (generic garment) | Label + tag only | Full — matches DROP 001 discipline |
| Construction claims allowed | **None** | **None** (unless you commission fabric specs) | Full, if tested |
| Time to first sale | 1–2 weeks | 4–6 weeks (sample + label lead time) | 8–12 weeks |
| Risk if it doesn't sell | Near zero | Moderate (batch + labels are sunk) | Higher (bigger batch) |

**Recommendation: start Tier A to validate demand with near-zero risk, graduate the
winning colourway(s) to Tier B within 60–90 days of positive sell-through, and only
consider Tier C once a set has proven repeat demand across two cycles.** This mirrors the
Model 1 → Model 2 discipline already established for DROP 001 — same logic, different range.

**Break-even ROAS ≈ 2.6** at Tier A — notably worse than DROP 001's core-range 1.9–2.1,
because dropship COGS and return rates are both structurally higher. This is the honest
trade for near-zero inventory risk. **Do not scale paid spend on this line until real
return-rate data replaces the 10% planning assumption** — resort/fit-sensitive categories
can run returns well above apparel norms if sizing isn't dialled in.

## 5. Marketing rules specific to this line (the honesty guardrail)

Because Tier A/B don't give us construction control, **this capsule may NOT use any
COASTFORM/GSM/reinforced-seam language from the core range.** Approved claim types instead:
fit description, styling versatility ("one set, five occasions"), colour/print story,
price-value, and — once real — genuine customer photos/reviews. This keeps the brand's
"no unsubstantiated claims" rule (`BRAND_BLUEPRINT.md` §10) intact even on a differently-
sourced line. Copy for this capsule leans on *occasion and versatility*, not fabric
engineering.

## 6. Red team — the pivot itself, scored

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation already built in |
|---|---|---|
| Undermines the "honest construction proof" brand pillar | High if mishandled | §5 claim restrictions; capsule framed as a lean-tested line, stated plainly on its own page |
| Dropship shipping times hurt trust (2–3 wk China transit) | Medium-High | Start Tier A with a supplier offering AU/US-held stock where possible; state honest delivery windows at checkout (same "no fake dates" rule as core range) |
| Quality variance / return spike | Medium | Order and personally test samples before listing (non-negotiable — see §7 step 2); publish real measurements once sample-verified |
| Category is commoditised/low-differentiation | Medium | Differentiation is brand voice + photography + Tide Check's brand-signature print, not price competition |
| Distracts from DROP 001 execution | Medium | Capped effort: 3 SKUs, Tier A only until DROP 001's Phase 4/5 (day 56+) per `90_DAY_PLAN.md` — this is a parallel low-effort test, not a second full launch |
| Margin is meaningfully worse than core range | Low-Medium (by design) | Priced and framed as the accessible entry line; core range remains the margin engine |

**Verdict: proceed, Tier A, capped scope, with the claims guardrail in §5 enforced in
code (see the landing page build) — not just written down.**

## 7. Step-by-step setup (do this in order)

1. **Pick 2–3 Tier-A supplier candidates** for camp-collar shirt + short sets on
   CJdropshipping / AppScenic-style marketplaces; filter for suppliers with AU or US-held
   inventory options to cut shipping time.
2. **Order and personally wear-test one sample of each candidate set** before listing
   anything — check fabric hand-feel, true-to-size fit, stitching, colourfastness on a
   real wash. This step is non-negotiable regardless of "automate everything" pressure —
   it's the only real QC gate you have at Tier A.
3. **Confirm real delivery windows** with the chosen supplier and use *only* those dates
   in checkout copy (never a rounded-down guess).
4. **List the 3 sets** using the honest claim rules in §5; use the landing page built in
   this repo (`store/src/pages/Riviera.jsx`) as the layout spec.
5. **Set a hard CAC ceiling** at $20/order (see §4 math) before spending on ads; validate
   organically first via founder/creator content ("testing a new set — here's the honest
   sourcing story") — the transparency angle is itself content, consistent with Engine One.
6. **Track sell-through weekly.** At 60–90 days, if a set is selling and returns are under
   ~8%, move that colourway to Tier B (private label) using the same supplier-vetting
   discipline as `MANUFACTURING_BRIEF.md`.
7. **Never let this line touch the DROP 001 supply chain, budget, or claims** — it's
   additive, not a replacement, until/unless it earns that by results.

## 8. Automation applied to this line

Order routing (Shopify → supplier via CJdropshipping/Zendrop native app — no manual order
entry), inventory sync (supplier stock levels pulled automatically so the site never
oversells), review-request flow (reuses `EMAIL_AND_SMS.md` Flow 12, unchanged), returns

sell-through/return-rate report auto-emailed to the founder. None of this replaces the
human sample-testing step in §7.2 — automation is for logistics, not quality judgment.
