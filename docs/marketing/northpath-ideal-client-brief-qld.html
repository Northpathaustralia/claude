Ideal Client & Lead Targeting Brief — Queensland

A clean summary of NorthPath's ideal-client research and location targeting data, prepared for the marketing team to plan and target lead-generation campaigns. This document contains targeting research only — it does not review or comment on any past or current marketing activity.
Prepared: July 2026 Focus market: Queensland Product: owner-occupier home purchase pathway

1. The Ideal Client at a Glance

Every campaign, audience and lead form should be built around this profile. A lead who matches all four headline criteria is exactly who we want to reach.

Superannuation
$150,000 minimum
Combined superannuation balance across the household.
Household income
$150,000+ combined
Own income plus partner income. Comfortably above this is even better.
Target purchase
$700k–$800k max
Established property, bought to live in (owner-occupier). Not new builds, not investment.
Credit history
Good / clean credit
No bankruptcies, Part IX agreements or significant unpaid defaults.

Supporting profile signals

Beyond the headline criteria, our lead research shows the strongest prospects also look like this:

SignalWhat "ideal" looks like
Age bandRoughly 30–55 years old.
EmploymentPAYG full-time, or self-employed with 2+ years of trading history. Part-time, casual and contract income can still qualify but ranks lower.
Unsecured debtTotal monthly repayments on cards, car loans, personal loans and buy-now-pay-later under about $1,000/month.
Buying timeframePlanning to buy within the next 2 years. Inside 12 months is the sweet spot; 0–6 months is hottest.
LocationLives in (or is targeting) a suburb where established houses commonly sell within the $700k–$800k ceiling — see Section 2.
Who we do not want campaigns to attract: bankruptcy or Part IX history, significant unpaid defaults, currently unemployed, buyers targeting property well above $800k, and pure investors (this product is for people buying an established home to live in).

3. How Leads Are Graded Once They Arrive

Every lead that comes in is scored out of 100 against the ideal client profile and graded A+ to D. Understanding the weighting helps you design lead forms and audiences that produce A-grade leads — the factors below are, in order, what makes a lead valuable to us.

Lead form fields that matter

The scoring engine needs these data points — the more of them a campaign captures, the more accurately we can prioritise the lead. Recommended fields for lead forms and landing pages:

  • Own annual income and partner annual income (or a combined household income band)
  • Employment type (PAYG full-time / part-time / casual, self-employed & years trading, contractor)
  • Approximate total monthly repayments on unsecured debt (cards, car/personal loans, BNPL)
  • Credit history self-assessment (clean / minor past issues / significant issues)
  • Buying timeframe (0–3, 3–6, 6–12 months, 1–2 years, 2+ years)
  • Suburb and postcode (postcode is the key targeting and scoring field)
  • Superannuation balance band (e.g. under $100k / $100–150k / $150k+)

4. Messaging Guardrails (Compliance)

All customer-facing marketing must follow these rules — they are non-negotiable:

  • Never promise approvals, finance or outcomes. Banned phrasing includes "guaranteed approval", "you will be approved / will qualify", "instant / automatic / assured approval", "pre-approved for sure", "no risk" and "100% approval/success".
  • Educate first, sell second. Content positions NorthPath as an education-and-preparation guide working alongside licensed professionals — NorthPath is not a lender.
  • Medians and affordability data are for targeting only — never quoted as valuations or price promises.
  • Append the standard disclaimer to customer-facing content: "This information is general and educational only. It does not consider your personal circumstances and is not financial or credit advice. Outcomes depend on individual circumstances — consider seeking advice from a licensed professional."
